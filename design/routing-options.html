The Routing section: five options

Each one is a section of its own under the mixer's strips, with the heading Routing. All five show the same project with the Drum bus selected: Kick and Snare play into it, it plays into the Pre-Master and on to Master, and Kick and Snare also send to Reverb. Pick one by its letter, or mix parts of two.

Main output Send, with its level The selected channel's own routes Everything else, quieter
A

Lines per destination

Every destination gets its own labelled line across the section. Each channel drops straight down from its strip onto the line of where it plays, in its own colour, and the line rises back into the destination's strip. It is the current schema, laid out with room to breathe.

Good forReading by strip: look under a channel, follow it down.
CostsOne line per destination; a big session needs the section to grow or scroll.
B

Signal flow, left to right

The sound's journey in columns: channels, then buses, then the Pre-Master, then Master. Curves link them in each channel's colour; the selected channel's whole path lights up from where its sound comes from to where it leaves.

Good forSeeing the order sound passes through, buses within buses included.
CostsNot lined up with the strips above; the eye moves between two layouts.
C

Patch grid

A grid with channels down the side and destinations across the top. A filled dot is a main output, a ring with a figure is a send at that level. The selected channel's row and column are lit, and a click on a cell routes or unroutes, as the route buttons do.

Good forExact answers at a glance, and the densest view of a big session.
CostsA table rather than a picture; flow through buses is read, not seen.
D

The selected channel's story

Only the selected channel, told plainly: one sentence, then what plays into it, the channel itself with its input, plugins and level, and every place it plays into, on to Master. Other channels appear as a quiet count.

Good forClarity: no crossing lines, and words a musician would say.
CostsShows one channel at a time; the whole mix is not in view.
E

Tree from Master

An outline with Master at the top and everything that feeds it nested below: the Pre-Master, then the buses with the channels inside them. Sends hang off the channel they go to, marked with their level. The selected branch is lit.

Good forGroups at a glance: what is inside the Drum bus is literally inside it.
CostsSends do not fit a tree well; they appear twice, once as a branch and once as a mark.
